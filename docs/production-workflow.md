# Gamantra Illustrated Film — Production Workflow

**Version:** 1.0  
**Purpose:** Create consistent, narrated illustrated films with minimal manual production work.

## 1. Story and narration

Prepare the final authored story and narration audio.

For short-form social media, begin with a working target of 45–90 seconds. Longer stories may have separate full-length versions.

The narration establishes the film's duration and verbal rhythm.

## 2. Visual storyboard

Create one complete visual storyboard showing every proposed illustration in narrative order.

The storyboard establishes:

- Scene composition and narrative purpose
- Character and environment continuity
- Objects, costumes, architecture, and visual details
- Artistic style, palette, and atmosphere
- Narrative surprises and reveal timing
- Opening title, subtitle, and closing credit

Present the storyboard as a numbered contact sheet for one consolidated review.

**Approval freezes the visual direction.**

## 3. Final illustration production

Produce each illustration using its corresponding approved storyboard panel as a direct visual reference.

Maintain shared character, environment, object, and palette references across the entire set.

Generate all final illustrations in vertical 9:16, preferably at 1080 × 1920 or higher.

No new scene content or conceptual reinterpretation should be introduced after storyboard approval.

## 4. Internal quality control

Before delivery, validate every illustration against:

- Its approved storyboard panel
- The established character and object designs
- The complete sequence for continuity
- The approved color palette
- The required aspect ratio and resolution
- The narrative order and preservation of surprises
- All required title and credit text

Correct demonstrable inconsistencies before delivery. Disclose any remaining limitations.

**Do not request intermediate reviews unless an unresolved issue genuinely requires an editorial decision.**

## 5. Final asset delivery

Deliver one versioned ZIP package containing:

- Sequentially named images: `scene-01.png`, `scene-02.png`, etc.
- A complete numbered contact sheet
- An image manifest mapping files to storyboard scenes
- A concise quality-control report

Freeze the accepted image package as **Art V1**.

Any subsequent change should be deliberate and versioned.

## 6. Remotion assembly

Use the frozen image set and original narration to construct the film.

Keep filmmaking instructions separate from illustrations, including:

- Exact narration-aligned scene boundaries
- Camera pans and zooms
- Reframing and image reuse
- Cuts and crossfades
- Opening and closing text treatment

Use an editable JSON timeline so that editing changes never require regenerating artwork.

## 7. Beat synchronization

Review important narration moments against the video timeline.

Adjust scene transitions to align with exact spoken words or pauses.

Retain freedom to change timing and camera choreography without disturbing the approved visual assets.

## 8. Render and release

Preview the finished film, render an MP4, and review the exported file.

Recommended vertical delivery format:

- Resolution: 1080 × 1920
- Aspect ratio: 9:16
- Frame rate: 30 fps
- Video: H.264
- Audio: AAC

Perform a final review for synchronization, visual cropping, audio quality, opening readability, and closing credits.

## Governing principle

**The storyboard establishes what we see. The final illustrations faithfully realize it. The camera determines how we experience it.**

Once the storyboard is approved, production should proceed with minimal review overhead.
